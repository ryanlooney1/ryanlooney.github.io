The "Sponsored" label hadn't been questioned since 2006

How I led the change across Meta's apps and generated $100M+ in annual revenue

The challenge

"Sponsored" had run on Meta ads since 2006. It's a long word that takes up valuable space. Over nearly two decades it became invisible infrastructure that was inherited, unquestioned, overlooked.

I saw an opening. At Meta's scale, simplifying the label to "Ad" was a rare opportunity to declutter the screen and increase ad transparency across billions of daily impressions.

Changing it was genuinely risky. Meta's FTC agreements explicitly reference "Sponsored", so changing it on Instagram required a global change across Facebook, Messenger, Threads, and WhatsApp too. Legal and policy teams had real concerns, there were country-specific translation problems to solve, and leadership wasn't convinced it was worth the effort. I spent 18 months making the case.

When content designers are usually handed a problem to solve, Ryan went looking for opportunity, built the case, and drove it to launch."
Netanis (Tanna) Waters, Content Design Leader, Meta

My approach

Made the business case

I made the case on three points: the FTC itself recommends "Ad" for clear disclosure, the shorter label cuts visual clutter (translations could be shortened from 9+ characters to 6+, on average), and user research confirmed people read "Sponsored" and "Ad" interchangeably. A simpler system with major revenue potential.

Navigated across apps

My team created a playbook to launch the new ad label, starting with Policy and Risk reviews and recruiting Facebook and cross-app partners into a global test. I led reviews and published plans to bring leadership along from start to finish.

Led global testing and rollout

I partnered with Instagram Monetization leads on a copy-only A/B test across ad surfaces, helped build the go-to-market plan, and coordinated translations and timeframes with teams across Meta. The new label launched in January 2026.

AI in practice

This wasn't an AI project, but I used AI tools to run it better. NotebookLM organized project documents and playbooks for my partner teams, helping Facebook and WhatsApp partners replicate my process and cite my rationale from Instagram.

Key insight

Default content like the "Ad" label is shared across teams and organizations. No single team or person owns default content, which is why it goes unquestioned and untested. The hard part, and my specialty, is building the case and gaining consensus that unquestioned content is worth time and investment.
